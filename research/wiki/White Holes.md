# White Holes

A white hole is the time-reversed Schwarzschild solution: a region of spacetime
from which matter and light can only emerge. It appears as region IV of the
maximal Kruskal–Szekeres extension.

## Geometry

Under $t\to-t$ the Schwarzschild metric is invariant, but the causal structure
differs. In Kruskal coordinates $U,V$ the four regions are: (I) exterior,
(II) black-hole interior, (III) parallel exterior, (IV) white-hole interior.
Region IV has a past singularity at $r=0$ and $r$ increases toward the future,
so nothing can enter from region I. The horizon $r=r_s$ is a null surface with
surface gravity $\kappa=c^4/(4GM)=c^2/(2r_s)$.

## Instability

White holes are catastrophically unstable. Perturbations obey the
Regge–Wheeler equation with reversed boundary conditions, and the instability
e-folding time is $\tau\sim 2r_s/c=4GM/c^3$, comparable to the free-fall time.
Mass inflation drives the interior mass to diverge on the same timescale. For a
solar-mass hole this is $\sim10^{-5}$ s; for a $10^9M_\odot$ hole, a few hours.

**Hawking comparison.** A 1 M☉ white hole (time-reversed Schwarzschild) has
Hawking temperature $T_H = \hbar c^3/(8\pi G M k_B) \approx 6.17\times10^{-8}$ K
and evaporation time $t_{\rm evap} \sim 5120\pi G^2 M^3/(\hbar c^4) \approx
2.1\times10^{67}$ yr. The ratio $t_{\rm evap}/\tau \sim 10^{80}$: classical
instability destroys the white hole 80 orders of magnitude before Hawking
evaporation could matter.

## Can they exist?

Classically, white holes are forbidden by the second law and by cosmic
censorship, and no known formation mechanism exists. Quantum mechanically, the
tunneling amplitude $\mathcal A\sim e^{-4\pi GM^2/\hbar c}$ is exponentially
suppressed for macroscopic masses. In loop quantum gravity the singularity
resolves into a quantum bounce and a black hole can transition to a white hole
(a "Planck star"), with bounce time $\sim(M/m_P)^{4/3}t_P$.

## Observational signatures and limits

Speculative signatures include white-hole explosions powering gamma-ray bursts
and fast radio bursts, with luminosity $L\sim c^5/(2G)\sim10^{52}$ W independent
of mass. CMB spectral distortions place an extremely tight limit on any
present-day population, $\rho_{\rm WH}\lesssim10^{-53}$ m$^{-3}$.
