# Gravitational Wave Analysis

Gravitational waves from compact binaries are the primary observational window
onto black holes. The waveform has three phases, each carrying different
information about the source.

## Inspiral, merger, ringdown

During inspiral the chirp mass
$\mathcal M=(m_1m_2)^{3/5}/(m_1+m_2)^{1/5}$ sets the frequency evolution,

$$f_{\rm GW}(t)=\frac{1}{8\pi}\left[\frac{5}{256}\frac{1}{\mathcal M^{5/3}}(t_{\rm coal}-t)\right]^{-3/8}.$$

At merger the peak luminosity briefly reaches $L_{\rm peak}\sim c^5/G\approx3.6\times10^{52}$ W.
The remnant settles to [[Kerr Metric]] through quasinormal-mode ringdown,
$\omega_{\rm QNM}\approx(c^3/GM)[1-0.63(1-a_*)^{3/10}]$. The no-hair theorem
predicts the ringdown spectrum is fixed by $M$ and $J$, so measuring multiple
modes tests the Kerr hypothesis. Parameter estimation uses the
[[Fisher Information Matrix]].

## Other messengers

The Event Horizon Telescope imaged the shadows of M87* ($6.5\times10^9M_\odot$,
$\sim42\,\mu$as) and Sgr A* ($4.0\times10^6M_\odot$, $\sim51.8\,\mu$as) at 230 GHz,
consistent with the photon-ring prediction $\theta_{\rm ring}\approx2.6R_s/D$.
X-ray spectroscopy of the broad Fe K$\alpha$ line and continuum fitting measure
spins, and high-frequency QPOs may track orbital motion at the ISCO. The full
detected-event sample is collected in [[Gravitational-Wave Catalogs]], and the
early-Universe growth of supermassive remnants is discussed in
[[Early Supermassive Black Hole Growth]]. Wormhole models predict echoes, a
signature of [[Wormholes]].
