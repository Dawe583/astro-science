# Black Hole Spectroscopy

Black hole spectroscopy tests the no-hair theorem by measuring quasi-normal
mode (QNM) frequencies in the ringdown of a merger. If a black hole is fully
described by mass $M$ and spin $a$, all QNM frequencies are functions of
$(M,a)$ only. Measuring two or more modes gives an overdetermined system;
consistency of the inferred $(M,a)$ from different modes tests the theorem.

## Quasi-normal modes

After a merger, the remnant black hole rings down by emitting gravitational
waves at discrete frequencies:

$$\omega_{\ell m n} = \frac{1}{M}\,f_{\ell m n}(a)$$

where $(\ell,m)$ are angular indices and $n$ is the overtone number. The
fundamental mode $(2,2,0)$ has $M\omega \approx 0.3737 - 0.0890\,i$ for
Schwarzschild. Higher overtones damp faster (larger imaginary part).

## The no-hair test

1. Fit the ringdown to a model with $N$ modes.
2. Infer $(M, a)$ from each mode independently.
3. If all modes give the same $(M, a)$ → no-hair theorem supported.
4. If they disagree → potential new physics or systematic errors.

## GW150914 analysis

Giesler et al. (2019) showed that including the $n=1$ overtone in the GW150914
ringdown yields a remnant mass/spin consistent with the full inspiral-merger-
ringdown analysis. This was the first observational evidence supporting the
no-hair theorem via overtones.

However, Cotesta et al. (2022) argued the overtone detection is not robust
against noise and waveform systematics. The debate is ongoing.

## Key results

- GW150914 (M ≈ 62 M☉, a ≈ 0.68): (2,2,0) at ~403 Hz, (2,2,1) at ~391 Hz
- Overtones improve the test by providing independent (M, a) constraints
- Higher overtones (n ≥ 2) are increasingly difficult to resolve
