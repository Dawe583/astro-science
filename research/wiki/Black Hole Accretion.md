# Black Hole Accretion

Accretion converts gravitational energy into radiation and is the main way black
holes are observed. The Eddington limit, the ISCO, and radiative efficiency set
the luminosity and growth rate.

## Eddington limit and efficiency

Balancing radiation pressure against gravity on ionized hydrogen gives

$$L_{\rm Edd} = \frac{4\pi GMm_pc}{\sigma_T}\approx1.26\times10^{38}\frac{M}{M_\odot}\ \mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}.$$

With radiative efficiency $\epsilon$, up to $\sim5.7\%$ of rest mass is radiated
at the Schwarzschild ISCO, and up to $\sim42\%$ for prograde orbits at the
extremal [[Kerr Metric]] ISCO. The mass growth rate is
$\dot M=(1-\epsilon)L/(\epsilon c^2)$; see
[[Early Supermassive Black Hole Growth]].

## Observational tracers

For stellar-mass black holes, the BlackCAT catalog compiles black-hole X-ray
binaries, and the broad Fe K$\alpha$ line profile and continuum fitting yield
spins (from $a_*\approx0$ in A0620-00 to $a_*\approx0.998$ in GRS 1915+105). For
supermassive black holes, AGN reverberation mapping uses broad-line response
delays and line velocities to estimate masses; the local compilation in this
workspace contains 86 nearby AGN and is method-comparison context, not an
early-Universe sample. Quasi-periodic oscillations may trace orbital motion at
the ISCO. Accretion also powers the electromagnetic output of the events in
[[Gravitational-Wave Catalogs]] and the emission around [[White Holes]] in
speculative models.
