# Detection Methods

Exoplanets are detected indirectly through their influence on host stars or
directly via imaging. Five methods dominate the current census.

## Transit

A planet crossing the stellar disk dims the star by

$$\delta = \left(\frac{R_p}{R_*}\right)^2.$$

Transit duration (impact parameter $b$):

$$T = \frac{P}{\pi}\frac{R_*}{a}\sqrt{1-b^2}.$$

Dominant method (Kepler, TESS); yields radius and, with RV, density.

## Radial velocity

The stellar reflex motion gives $K = (2\pi G/P)^{1/3} M_p\sin i/(M_*+M_p)^{2/3}$,
yielding minimum mass $M_p\sin i$.

## Direct imaging

Spatially resolved planets at wide separations; contrast-limited, favours young,
massive planets. Coronagraphs and starshades suppress stellar glare.

## Microlensing

A foreground lens magnifies a background source; the Einstein radius sets the
mass and distance. Sensitive to low-mass planets at large separations. The
deflection is a weak-field limit of [[Geodesic Equations]].

## Astrometry

Orbital inclination from the star's astrometric wobble; Gaia is expanding this
sample. Parameter uncertainties are forecast with the [[Fisher Information Matrix]].
