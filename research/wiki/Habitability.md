# Habitability

Whether a planet can sustain liquid water and life depends on its orbit,
atmosphere, and host star. The conservative habitable zone (HZ) is bounded by
the runaway greenhouse (inner) and maximum greenhouse (outer) limits.

## Habitable zone

Kopparapu et al. (2013) give the distance

$$d = \sqrt{\frac{L_*/L_\odot}{S_{\rm eff}}}\ {\rm AU},$$

with $S_{\rm eff}$ a polynomial in $T=T_{\rm eff}-5780$ K. For the Sun the
inner edge is $\approx 0.95$ AU and the outer $\approx 1.68$ AU. See
`premium/theory_habitability.md` for the coefficient table.

## Tidal locking

Close-in planets are often 1:1 tidally locked (rotation period = orbital
period); Mercury occupies a 3:2 spin-orbit resonance. Locking reshapes climate
and atmospheric circulation, and drives tidal heating that can sustain
subsurface oceans.

## Atmospheric escape

Jeans escape is governed by $\lambda=v_{\rm esc}^2/v_{\rm th}^2$; escape is
significant for $\lambda\lesssim 10$–$15$. Energy-limited and hydrodynamic
escape can strip H/He envelopes, shaping the radius valley. See
[[Atmospheric Characterization]] and `premium/theory_atmospheric_escape.md`.

## Biosignatures

Candidate gases: O$_2$, CH$_4$, H$_2$O, CO$_2$, O$_3$. Disequilibrium pairs
(O$_2$+CH$_4$) are stronger than single gases, but abiotic false positives
(CO$_2$ photolysis, serpentinization) require contextual interpretation.

## Greenhouse limits

Runaway greenhouse (inner edge), moist greenhouse (water loss to space), and
maximum greenhouse (CO$_2$ condensation, outer edge) define HZ boundaries.
