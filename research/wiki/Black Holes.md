# Black Holes

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing,
not even light, can escape. The boundary is the **event horizon**; the interior
contains a singularity (for Schwarzschild/Kerr) shielded by the horizon. The
full derivation of every solution below is in `premium/theory_black_holes.md`.

## The four exact solutions

- **[[Schwarzschild Metric]]** — static, uncharged, spherically symmetric.
  Mass $M$ only; horizon at $r_s=2GM/c^2$; ISCO at $6M$, photon sphere at
  $3M$.
- **[[Reissner-Nordström Metric]]** — static, charged ($Q$). Horizons at
  $r_\pm = M \pm \sqrt{M^2-Q^2}$ (geometric units); extremal when $|Q|=M$.
- **[[Kerr Metric]]** — rotating (spin $a=J/M$), uncharged. Ergosphere,
  frame dragging, ISCO from $6M$ (Schwarzschild) down to $M$ (extremal
  prograde). Rotational energy up to $\sim 29\%$ of $M$ is extractable.
- **[[Kerr-Newman Metric]]** — rotating and charged; the most general
  stationary black-hole solution. ADM mass, angular momentum and charge are the
  only externally measurable parameters (no-hair theorem).

## Key properties

- **Event horizon / ergosphere**: the Kerr ergosphere (between $r_+$ and the
  static limit) allows negative-energy orbits and the Penrose process.
- **Singularity theorems** ([[Raychaudhuri Equation]]): under reasonable
  energy conditions, gravitational collapse inevitably produces a singularity.
- **Hawking radiation**: a black hole of mass $M$ has temperature
  $T = \hbar c^3/(8\pi G M k_B) \approx 6.17\times 10^{-8}\,\text{K}$ for
  $M=1\,M_\odot$; it evaporates on a timescale $\propto M^3$.
- **No-hair theorem**: stationary black holes are fully described by
  $(M, J, Q)$.

## Energy extraction

- Penrose process (single decay): up to $\sim 20.7\%$ of the infalling
  particle's energy (see [[Remnant Energetics]]).
- Rotational reservoir (Christodoulou–Ruffini): up to $\sim 29.3\%$ of $M$.
- Blandford–Znajek (magnetic) is the astrophysical analogue.
