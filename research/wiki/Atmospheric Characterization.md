# Atmospheric Characterization

Exoplanet atmospheres are probed by transmission and emission spectroscopy,
revealing composition, temperature structure, and escape.

## Scale height

The isothermal scale height is

$$H = \frac{k_B T}{\mu m_p g},\qquad g=\frac{G M_p}{R_p^2}.$$

## Transmission spectroscopy

A transiting planet's apparent radius grows by $\sim H$; the transit-depth
variation is

$$\frac{\Delta F}{F} = \frac{2 R_p H}{R_*^2}.$$

Molecular absorption (H$_2$O, CO$_2$, CH$_4$, O$_3$) modulates this depth with
wavelength, enabling retrieval of abundances and temperature.

## Emission and thermal phase curves

Dayside emission spectra and phase curves constrain albedo, heat redistribution,
and atmospheric circulation, including for tidally locked planets (see
[[Habitability]]).

## Escape signatures

Lyman-$\alpha$ and metal-line absorption during transit trace hydrodynamic
escape; mass-loss rates follow the energy-limited formula. See
`premium/theory_atmospheric_escape.md`.

## Retrieval

Bayesian retrievals infer composition and profiles from spectra; degeneracies
between clouds, metallicity, and C/O ratio are common. JWST and Ariel are the
current workhorses.
