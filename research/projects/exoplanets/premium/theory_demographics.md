# Demographics and Occurrence Rates

Self-contained overview of exoplanet demographics.

## 1. Mass-radius relation

Rocky: $R \propto M^{0.28}$ (Zeng et al. 2016). Volatile-rich: $R \propto M^{0.5}$–$M^{0.7}$.
Transition at $\sim 1.5$–$1.8\,R_\oplus$.

## 2. Radius valley (Fulton gap)

Gap at $\sim 1.5$–$2.0\,R_\oplus$ caused by atmospheric mass loss:
- Photoevaporation: XUV-driven, mass-loss rate $\dot M \propto F_{\rm XUV} R_p^3 / (M_p K)$
- Core-powered: thermal cooling, energy from formation

## 3. Occurrence rates

Fraction of stars with planets in a given period-radius bin. Depends on stellar metallicity, mass, and age. Hot Jupiters: $\sim 1\%$ of Sun-like stars. Super-Earths: $\sim 30$–$50\%$.

## 4. Metallicity dependence

Giant planet occurrence increases with metallicity: $P({\rm giant}) \propto 10^{2[{\rm Fe/H}]}$.
Rocky planet occurrence is less metallicity-dependent.
