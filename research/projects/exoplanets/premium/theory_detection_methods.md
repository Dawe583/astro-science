# Detection Methods

Self-contained overview of exoplanet detection methods with key equations.

## 1. Transit

Flux drop: $\delta = (R_p/R_*)^2$. Duration: $T = (P/\pi)(R_*/a)\sqrt{1-b^2}$.
Impact parameter $b = (a/R_*)\cos i$. Requires edge-on orbit ($i \approx 90°$).

## 2. Radial velocity

Stellar reflex velocity: $K = (2\pi G/P)^{1/3} \frac{M_p \sin i}{(M_*+M_p)^{2/3}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-e^2}}$.
Measures $M_p \sin i$ (minimum mass). Dominant for massive, close-in planets.

## 3. Direct imaging

Contrast: $\Delta m = -2.5\log_{10}(F_p/F_*)$. Requires large separation and young, hot planets. Coronagraphs block starlight.

## 4. Microlensing

Einstein radius: $R_E = \sqrt{4GM D_{LS}/(c^2 D_L D_S)}$. Planet detection via caustic crossings. Sensitive to cold, distant planets.

## 5. Astrometry

Angular semi-major axis: $\alpha = (M_p/M_*) (a/D)$. Gaia can detect Jupiter-mass planets at AU separations.

## 6. TTV

Transit timing variations: gravitational perturbations from companion planets cause deviations from linear ephemeris. Sensitive to low-mass, resonant systems.
